5 things to love about Sheffield

1. Theatres, music and culture

Sheffield has the largest theatre district outside London, a thriving local music scene, and a busy calendar of festivals covering: writing, documentary films, podcasts, political debate, and the culture of migration.

2. UK foodie capital

Thought UK food was boring? Not in Sheffield. Our independent food and restaurant scene is thriving, with a huge range of options: international cuisine, busy food halls, Michelin star dining, local restaurants and artisan bakeries.

3. Green and outdoor spaces

61% of the city is green space, making Sheffield the 2nd greenest city in Europe (after Oslo). The Peak District National Park’s countryside is just a short bus or train ride away - it’s so close you can even walk, run or cycle there.

4. The shopping

Meadowhall, one of the UK’s biggest shopping centres and home to all the big brands, is a quick tram or train ride away. The city centre and surrounding neighbourhoods are home to a wide range of independent shops, local makers, and sellers of vintage clothing.

5. Live well for less

The cost of living in Sheffield is much lower than London and other major UK cities. Your budget goes further here and the Sheffield lifestyle means you don’t have to spend much to enjoy your time away from studying.

Watch: Sheffield city tour

Will I feel at home?

Sheffield is a modern multicultural city that’s home to people from many different nationalities, ethnicities and religions.

The city also has a long tradition of welcoming refugees and asylum seekers, becoming the first official City of Sanctuary in the UK.

With such diverse communities, and lots of international supermarkets and restaurants, it’s not surprising that so many international students feel at home here:

“One of the first things that I noticed about Sheffield was its international character. You can see people from all around the world which is something that I didn’t expect really and I got the chance to make friends with students from all around the world and learn their culture and customs.”

Eleni Koursari, Cyprus, MSc Antimicrobial Resistance
